**Task A: Portfolio analysis**

  https://thanhvtran.com/

  Initially, the portfolio is well organized, and a reader can locate any project or internship the owner has completed relatively quickly, making it accessible for anyone looking to review the author’s experience.
  Unfortunately, when looking through the projects and student teams, while they are well documented with images, the portfolio lacks sufficient technical documentation. The projects contain only brief summaries of what they were, what was done, and what was accomplished. Because of this, a colleague would not have enough information to reproduce the work without asking additional questions. Additionally, the portfolio does not sufficiently showcase how decisions were made throughout each project, focusing primarily on the final product rather than the reasoning and design process that led to it. 
  On the other hand, the language is appropriate for an employer-facing portfolio and maintains a professional tone throughout most of the website. There are a few more relaxed sections on the home page, which, because they are not used throughout the entire portfolio, provide some personality without detracting from the overall presentation. 
  It is also worth mentioning that the portfolio has a sophisticated visual design. The background videos, overall organization, and numerous images provide visual representation of each section and make the portfolio engaging to explore. 
  Overall, the portfolio is effective at summarizing the author’s projects, internships, and other accomplishments, but it is less effective as a technical record because it does not provide enough documentation or reasoning for the projects to be independently reproduced. 

**Task B: Product Analysis**

  **Product chosen:** Combination wrench
  
**a)** The primary function of this product is to transform an applied force into torque, using mechanical advantage, to tighten or loosen an external fastener. 

**b)** The physical principle that governs its behavior is torque.

τ = F × r × sin(θ)

where:

  τ (Tau): torque (N·m) 
  F: force applied (N) 
  r: perpendicular distance from the pivot point to the point where the force is applied (m) 
  θ (Theta): angle between the force line and the lever arm 

One assumption that makes this model valid is that the wrench is sufficiently rigid, allowing the deformation caused by the applied force to be neglected.
